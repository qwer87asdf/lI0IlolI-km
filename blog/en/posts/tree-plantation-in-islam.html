Tree plantation in Islam

Shafayat · 3 Aug 2026 · 10 min read

Plants Are a Blessing from Allah

Plants, trees, and all forms of vegetation are among the countless blessings of Allah Almighty. They are indispensable for maintaining the balance and harmony of the earth. Whether humans or animals, the primary source of food for all living beings comes from plants; they make the earth lush and green. Trees help reduce the planet’s rising temperature. Weary travelers find cool shade beneath them. Their branches and dry leaves serve as firewood. Large trees are cut into timber and transformed into beautiful furniture. The leaves of some trees, the fruits of others, and even the bark of certain trees are used as medicine or as raw materials for producing medicine. And these are only a few of the countless benefits of trees, plants, and vines.


This Blessing Mentioned in the Holy Qur’an

The Holy Qur’an repeatedly draws our attention to this great blessing bestowed by Allah. Consider a few examples:

فَلْیَنْظُرِ الْاِنْسَانُ اِلٰى طَعَامِهٖۤۙ ۝۲۴ اَنَّا صَبَبْنَا الْمَآءَ صَبًّاۙ ۝۲۵ ثُمَّ شَقَقْنَا الْاَرْضَ شَقًّاۙ ۝۲۶ فَاَنْۢبَتْنَا فِیْهَا حَبًّاۙ ۝۲۷ وَّ عِنَبًا وَّ قَضْبًاۙ ۝۲۸ وَّ زَیْتُوْنًا وَّ نَخْلًاۙ ۝۲۹ وَّ حَدَآىِٕقَ غُلْبًاۙ ۝۳۰ وَّ فَاكِهَةً وَّ اَبًّاۙ ۝۳۱ مَّتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَ لِاَنْعَامِكُمْؕ ۝۳۲

Translation: “Let man then look at his food. Indeed, We poured down water in abundance. Then We split the earth open, causing grain to grow from it, along with grapes, vegetables, olives, date palms, dense gardens, fruits, and grass, as a provision for you and for your livestock.”

(Surah ‘Abasa, Verses 24–32)

These verses mention a remarkable variety of plants. They speak of towering trees such as date palms, climbing vines like grapes, different kinds of grains and vegetables, and even the grass that appears to grow on its own without human care.

For all these plants to grow, and even for a tiny seed to sprout, Allah Almighty sends rain down from the heavens. With just a touch of rainwater, hard earth splits apart, allowing tender green shoots to emerge. Is this alone not sufficient evidence of Allah’s infinite power?

In these verses, Allah directs human beings to reflect upon their food. Where does this endless variety of nourishment come from? By whose command do plants grow? Who creates the conditions that allow grass, trees, and crops to flourish?

Although these blessings surround us every day, each one is a profound sign of Allah’s limitless power and mercy. The Holy Qur’an presents another remarkable example:

وَ اٰیَةٌ لَّهُمُ الْاَرْضُ الْمَیْتَةُ ۖ ۚ اَحْیَیْنٰهَا وَ اَخْرَجْنَا مِنْهَا حَبًّا فَمِنْهُ یَاْكُلُوْنَ ۝۳۳ وَ جَعَلْنَا فِیْهَا جَنّٰتٍ مِّنْ نَّخِیْلٍ وَّ اَعْنَابٍ وَّ فَجَّرْنَا فِیْهَا مِنَ الْعُیُوْنِۙ ۝۳۴ لِیَاْكُلُوْا مِنْ ثَمَرِهٖ ۙ وَ مَا عَمِلَتْهُ اَیْدِیْهِمْ ؕ اَفَلَا یَشْكُرُوْنَ ۝۳۵ سُبْحٰنَ الَّذِیْ خَلَقَ الْاَزْوَاجَ كُلَّهَا مِمَّا تُنْۢبِتُ الْاَرْضُ وَ مِنْ اَنْفُسِهِمْ وَ مِمَّا لَا یَعْلَمُوْنَ ۝۳۶

Translation: A sign for them is the dead earth. We give it life and bring forth from it grain, from which they eat. We have placed therein gardens of date palms and grapevines, and caused springs to gush forth, so that they may eat from their fruits. Yet it was not their hands that produced them. Will they not then give thanks? Glory be to the One Who created all things in pairs: what the earth produces, what they themselves are, and even what they do not yet know.

(Surah Ya-Sin, Verses 33–36)

The words, “Yet it was not their hands that produced them. Will they not then give thanks?”, deserve special attention. In these verses, Allah Almighty makes it abundantly clear that barren and lifeless land is revived by the rain He sends down from the heavens. The hardened earth splits apart, tender shoots emerge from within, and in time those shoots become fields of grain, flourishing grapevines, or towering date palms. Eventually, people harvest their fruits and consume them as their sustenance. Yet this provision is not the creation of human hands.

A person may plant a small sapling or sow a seed in the soil, but that is the extent of human ability. Does anyone possess the power to nurture that seed into a flourishing tree adorned with flowers and laden with fruit? Certainly not. It is Allah Almighty alone Who brings about its growth. This is exactly what He makes clear in these verses: the provisions we enjoy are not the work of our own hands. They are blessings from Allah, manifestations of His infinite mercy and His perfect creation.

Grafted saplings can be planted throughout the year. However, the ideal season for tree plantation is during the months of June, July, and August. Let us plant improved varieties of saplings and contribute to a greener, healthier world.

The Holy Qur’an makes this reality even clearer in another verse:

اَفَرَءَیْتُمْ مَّا تَحْرُثُوْنَؕ ۝۶۳ ءَاَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُوْنَهٗۤ اَمْ نَحْنُ الزّٰرِعُوْنَ ۝۶۴

Translation: “Have you considered what you sow? Is it you who make it grow, or are We the One Who causes it to grow?”

(Surah Al-Waqi’ah, Verses 63–64)

In other words, do we truly possess the power to make the seeds we sow split open and sprout into living plants? All we can do is place a seed into the soil with our own hands. Nothing more. At most, we can transplant a young sapling from one place to another. Beyond that, we have no power whatsoever. We cannot command a seed to germinate, breathe life into it, or make it grow. That extraordinary process belongs to Allah alone.

Tree Plantation in the Light of the Hadith

Allah Almighty nurtures the seeds we sow and the saplings we plant, allowing them to grow into flourishing trees adorned with flowers and fruit. From them, we receive our sustenance. By itself, this is already an immense blessing. Yet the mercy of Allah does not end there. Through these trees, He provides us with food, helps maintain the balance of our environment, offers us cool shade, and grants us countless other benefits. These are rewards we enjoy in this world. Beyond all of these, however, Allah has also reserved everlasting rewards in the Hereafter for those who plant trees. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

مَا مِنْ مُسْلِمٍ يَغْرِسُ غَرْسًا ، أَوْ يَزْرَعُ زَرْعًا فَيَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ طَيْرٌ ، أَوْ إِنْسَانٌ ، أَوْ بَهِيمَةٌ إِلاَّ كَانَ لَهُ بِهِ صَدَقَةٌ

Translation: “Whenever a Muslim plants a tree or cultivates a crop, and a bird, a person, or an animal eats from it, it is counted as an act of charity (Sadaqah) for him.”

(Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 2320)

Tree Plantation in Islam is not merely an act of cultivating the earth; it is an act of worship that continues to benefit both the planter and others. People usually plant trees to enjoy their fruits themselves or to provide for their families in the future. Naturally, one expects to eat the fruit of a tree that one has planted with one’s own hands. There is nothing unusual about that. Yet the beauty of this teaching is that even when the planter himself enjoys its fruits, or when any other person benefits from them, every such benefit is recorded by Allah as an act of charity on behalf of the one who planted the tree.

Human beings are the noblest of Allah’s creation. Yet even if a grazing animal eats from that tree, or a bird pecks at its fruit, reducing what the owner might have harvested, the planter does not lose his reward. Instead, Allah records it as an act of charity for him.

Even more remarkable is another narration of the Prophet ﷺ. He taught that if someone steals the fruit from a tree, the owner and the one who planted it are still rewarded. The Hadith states:

مَا مِنْ مُسْلِمٍ يَغْرِسُ غَرْسًا إِلاَّ كَانَ مَا أُكِلَ مِنْهُ لَهُ صَدَقَةٌ وَمَا سُرِقَ مِنْهُ لَهُ صَدَقَةٌ وَمَا أَكَلَ السَّبُعُ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ لَهُ صَدَقَةٌ وَمَا أَكَلَتِ الطَّيْرُ فَهُوَ لَهُ صَدَقَةً وَلاَ يَرْزَؤُهُ أَحَدٌ إِلاَّ كَانَ لَهُ صَدَقَةٌ

Translation: “Whenever a Muslim plants a tree, whatever is eaten from it is counted as charity for him; whatever is stolen from it is counted as charity for him; whatever is eaten by wild animals is counted as charity for him; whatever is eaten by birds is counted as charity for him; and whatever anyone takes from it is also counted as charity for him.”

(Sahih Muslim, Hadith 1552)

Just imagine how many different ways a single tree can become a source of Sadaqah. Feeding another person earns reward. Eating from its fruits yourself also earns reward. Even if someone steals its fruits without permission, or birds and animals consume them before you do, Allah still records every one of those losses as charity in your account.

What is even more extraordinary is that this reward is not limited to a person’s lifetime. If someone plants a tree and then passes away, as long as that tree continues to bear fruit and benefit Allah’s creation, its planter continues to receive reward. In this sense, planting a tree becomes a form of Sadaqah Jariyah—a continuous charity whose rewards endure long after one’s life has come to an end.

Tree Plantation Is an Act of Service

Tree Plantation in Islam is not limited to planting fruit-bearing trees for the reward of Sadaqah. Besides this, there are many other ways in which planting trees earns reward. If someone plants a shade tree beside a road, and it eventually grows large enough to provide cool shade for travelers, he will also be rewarded. Providing comfort to weary travelers through its shade is itself a source of reward.

Tree Plantation in Islam also encourages believers to plant trees with the sincere intention of benefiting others. For example, by planting fruit trees, one may intend to feed people with their fruits. If it is another type of tree, it may later provide useful materials such as timber or firewood. Likewise, a farmer may cultivate crops as his profession and earn his livelihood by selling them, yet he may also intend to benefit others through his work. A believer is rewarded according to his intention.

Another important aspect of this intention is that trees are among the most effective means of reducing global warming. People throughout the world benefit from this. A single tree planted by one person may not reduce the temperature of the entire planet, but if tree planting becomes a movement across the world, it will certainly make a significant contribution to reducing global temperatures. Not only human beings, but animals and birds will also benefit from it. Undoubtedly, every creature belongs to the family of Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said:

الْخَلْقُ عِيَالُ اللهِ، وَأَحَبُّ الْعِبَادِ إِلَى اللهِ أَنْفَعُهُمْ لِعِيَالِهِ

Translation: “All creation is the family of Allah, and the most beloved of Allah’s servants are those who are most beneficial to His creation.”

(Shu’ab al-Iman, Hadith 7045)

Therefore, tree plantation can become a unique means of serving Allah’s creation. Let this social movement begin today. It may be difficult for one person, or even ten people, to plant ten million saplings. But it is not difficult for ten million people. So let us plant trees, serve Allah’s creation, and protect the environment.

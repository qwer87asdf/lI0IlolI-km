The Story of Bahira the Monk and the Sahabi Tree: What Really Happened

Hasan · 30 Jul 2026 · 9 min read

You must have heard the story of the tree that bowed its branches to shade a young boy, and the monk who recognized something remarkable in him – let’s discuss it again, this time in full detail.

The desert sun beat down on a slow-moving caravan making its way north from Mecca toward the trading towns of Syria. Dust rose behind camels laden with goods, and among the travelers walked a boy no older than twelve, staying close to his uncle. He had no way of knowing that this journey – and a brief encounter beneath the shade of a tree later remembered as the Sahabi Tree – would become one of the most retold stories in Islamic tradition.

This is the story of Bahira the monk, the young Muhammad ﷺ, and the ancient tree said to still stand today in the deserts of modern-day Jordan.

The Story in Brief

  • As a young boy of about 12, Muhammad ﷺ traveled with his uncle Abu Talib on a trade caravan from Mecca to Syria.
  • Along the route, near the town of Busra, the caravan rested beneath a tree now popularly known as the Sahabi Tree.
  • A Christian monk named Bahira, who lived nearby and was known for his religious knowledge, noticed unusual signs – including a cloud shading the boy and the tree bending its branches over him.
  • Bahira examined the boy and reportedly recognized signs matching descriptions of a future prophet found in older religious texts.
  • He warned Abu Talib to protect the boy carefully, particularly from those who might wish him harm, especially the Jews of that region who he feared might recognize the same signs.
  • The Sahabi Tree, still standing near Al-Karak, Jordan, is popularly associated with this event, though the historical certainty of the exact tree’s identity is debated.
  • The story itself comes from early Islamic biographical (seerah) literature, and while widely told, scholars have raised questions about certain details and the strength of its chain of narration.

Setting the Scene: A Boy Raised Under Difficult Circumstances

To understand this story, it helps to remember the circumstances of Muhammad’s ﷺ early life. He was born in Mecca around 570 CE, and by the time he was six years old, he had lost both of his parents. He was first raised by his grandfather, Abdul Muttalib, and after his grandfather’s death, his uncle Abu Talib took on the responsibility of raising him.

Abu Talib was a respected merchant, and like many in Mecca at the time, his livelihood depended on trade caravans that traveled long distances – often to Syria (Sham), a major hub of commerce in the region. When Muhammad ﷺ was around twelve years old, Abu Talib decided to take him along on one such journey, reportedly because the boy was reluctant to be separated from his uncle.

The Caravan Reaches Busra

The journey from Mecca to Syria was long, cutting through arid desert landscapes and passing through various trading towns along the way. According to the traditional account – recorded by early Islamic historians such as Ibn Ishaq and referenced in later hadith collections like Sunan al-Tirmidhi – the caravan eventually reached the outskirts of Busra, a town in what is now southern Syria, near the border of modern-day Jordan.

It was here, in this region, that the caravan is said to have stopped to rest beneath a large, shaded tree – the very tree later remembered in local tradition as the Sahabi Tree.

Nearby lived a Christian monk named Bahira, known in the area for his deep religious learning. He lived a solitary, devoted life, and travellers passing through often knew of him, though he rarely engaged much with passing caravans.

Bahira Notices Something Unusual

According to the traditional account, Bahira noticed something strange as the caravan approached and settled beneath the tree. Though he had seen countless caravans pass by over the years without paying them much attention, this time something was different.

He reportedly observed a small cloud that seemed to move and provide shade specifically over the young boy as the group traveled, and – as the caravan rested – the branches of the Sahabi Tree appeared to bend and lower themselves, extending greater shade directly over Muhammad ﷺ, even though he had settled beneath it after the others.

Intrigued and unsettled by what he was witnessing, Bahira, breaking from his usual habits, invited the entire caravan to a meal – a gesture rarely extended, and one clearly meant to give him an opportunity to observe the boy more closely.

The Examination

When the caravan arrived for the meal, Bahira reportedly noticed that the young boy had stayed behind with the belongings rather than joining the group. Sensing this was significant, he asked that no one be excluded from the invitation, and Muhammad ﷺ was brought to join them.

As the story goes, Bahira watched the boy closely throughout the gathering, and afterward, he asked Abu Talib for permission to examine him further. According to the accounts, Bahira looked for physical signs he associated with descriptions of a coming prophet found in earlier religious scriptures – reportedly including a distinctive mark between the boy’s shoulders, sometimes referred to in Islamic tradition as the “seal of prophethood.”

Bahira then asked the young boy a series of questions – about his habits, his dreams, and aspects of his daily life – and is said to have found the boy’s answers consistent with what he understood from religious texts describing a future prophet.

The Warning to Abu Talib

After completing his observations, Bahira is reported to have turned to Abu Talib with striking seriousness. He asked what relation the boy was to him, and upon learning Abu Talib was his uncle, Bahira gave him a direct and urgent piece of advice: to take the boy back home and protect him carefully, warning that if certain people – particularly some among the Jewish communities of the region who might recognize similar prophetic signs from their own scriptures – were to identify him, they might intend him harm.

Taking this warning seriously, Abu Talib is said to have shortened the trip and returned home with the boy sooner than originally planned, out of concern for his safety.

What Historians and Scholars Say

While this story is one of the most well-known accounts from the early life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, it’s important to look at it with appropriate historical context.

The narrative appears in early seerah (biographical) literature, most notably in the work of Ibn Ishaq, one of the earliest biographers of the Prophet ﷺ, and is also referenced with some variation in later hadith collections. However, scholars of hadith have noted that certain chains of narration for this specific story contain weaknesses, and some details – particularly the more elaborate or embellished versions that developed over time – are viewed with caution by many classical and contemporary scholars.

That said, the core outline of the story – a young Muhammad ﷺ traveling to Syria with Abu Talib, and an encounter with a Christian monk who recognized something remarkable about him – is widely accepted as part of the broader historical memory of the Prophet’s ﷺ early life, even as scholars differ on the precise details and the authenticity of specific narrated elements.

This kind of nuance is common in early Islamic history – distinguishing between the general, well-attested outline of events and specific narrated details that may carry varying degrees of certainty.

The Sahabi Tree Today

Remarkably, a very old tree still stands today near Al-Karak, Jordan, popularly identified by local tradition as the same Sahabi Tree described in this story. It has become something of a local landmark, occasionally visited by travellers and those curious about early Islamic history passing through the region. However, it’s worth being clear-eyed here: while the tree is genuinely ancient and the local tradition connecting it to this story is long-standing, definitively verifying that this exact tree is the one described in 6th-century accounts is not something that can be established with certainty through historical or botanical evidence alone. It remains, more accurately, a site of strong local tradition and popular reverence rather than a scientifically confirmed historical relic.

Why This Story Still Resonates

Regardless of the exact historical details, the story of Bahira and the Sahabi Tree continues to hold meaning for Muslims as part of the broader narrative of the Prophet’s ﷺ early life – a life marked, even before prophethood formally began, by moments that those around him found remarkable and difficult to fully explain.

It also reflects an important theme found throughout Islamic tradition: the idea that signs of truth were present and recognized by sincere, knowledgeable people from other faith backgrounds – long before the message of Islam was formally revealed.

Ending Notes…

Whether examined as history, tradition, or an early sign pointing toward what was to come, it remains one of the most enduring and thought-provoking accounts from his early life, remembered still today beneath the shade of a tree in the Jordanian desert.

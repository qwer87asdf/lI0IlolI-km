One Month, 61 Million Reminders: Contributors Impact Throughout the July 2026

Shafayat · 17 Aug 2026 · 8 min read

Assalamu ‘alaikum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh.

The Ansar of Madinah did not lead the caravan. They opened their homes to it. Because of them, a message survived, spread, and reached across the world. Every person who has ever given, in any amount, to keep the Muslims Day app running stands in that same tradition today. You do not appear on any screen in this app. No user will ever know your name. And yet nothing you are about to read would have happened without you.

This is an account of what your support made possible over the past 31 days – and a reminder of why quiet, sustained giving matters more than most of us realize.

What You Made Possible

In just 31 days, Muslims Day carried the call to prayer 61 million times to 1.1 million Muslims around the world.

Sit with that number for a moment. Sixty-one million reminders work out to roughly 2.18 million every single day – about fifty-five for every person who uses the app. Somewhere in Dhaka, in Riyadh, in Paris, a phone buzzed at Fajr, and someone got up who might otherwise have slept through it. Multiply that moment by 61 million, and you start to see the shape of what quiet generosity actually builds.

Here is the fuller picture of what happened in a single month:

Taken together, the people this community serves spent more than 480,000 hours inside this app in a single month. That is over 20,000 days – more than fifty-four years of human time, lived inside one month, spent turning toward prayer, Qur’an, and du’a. Fifty-four years of attention pointed toward Allah, made possible by people most users will never meet and never thank by name.

“So establish the prayer. Indeed, the prayer has been appointed at fixed times.” (Meaning of Surah an-Nisa’ 4:103)

Acts of Prayer, Not Statistics

It is easy for numbers like these to blur together into an abstraction. So here is what those numbers actually were, broken down over the past 28 days:

Behind each row is a person, not a data point. The 153,000 who read Qur’an inside the app opened it 1.4 million times – roughly nine sittings each. For many of them, this app is the only Qur’an they carry with them, tucked into a pocket between shifts, between train stops, between the ordinary demands of a day that leaves little room for anything else.

The 126,000 who reached for du’a did so 2.8 million times – about twenty-two times each. These are not casual taps. These are people looking for the right words at a hard moment, and finding them, at 2am or in a hospital waiting room or on a walk they needed to take alone.

And 186,000 people deliberately set an alarm for prayer. That is not a passive download sitting unused on a home screen. That is a decision, made again and again, to be woken.

“Whoever guides another to something good receives a reward like the one who acts upon it.” (Meaning of a hadith reported in Sahih Muslim)

Sixty-one million reminders were sixty-one million small acts of guidance toward something good. What is written for the people who made those reminders possible is with Allah alone – but the hope contained in that is not small.

But the app has travelled far beyond home. In a single recent week it was used across India, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, France, Germany, and Brazil.

Think about who those people actually are. A construction worker in the Gulf, far from his family, with no masjid within hearing distance. Not a Download. A Daily Habit.

It is easy to install an app and forget it exists a week later. That is not what is happening here.

More than one in three monthly users opens Muslims Day on any given day. Very few apps in the world come close to that kind of return rate. It means this has stopped being software people happened to install and has become part of the actual rhythm of people’s prayer – which is precisely what a tool built for prayer should be.

At the exact moment this report was compiled, around 7,900 Muslims were using the app in that same instant. Nearly seven thousands of them were in Bangladesh.

There was one day in particular worth remembering: on 21 July 2026, 959,014 people used Muslims Day in a single day – more than the app’s own forecasting systems believed was statistically possible. The system flagged it as an anomaly. It is easier, and truer, to read it as a mercy. Around that same week, usage across Bangladesh rose by 119% compared to the week before.

Free for Every Muslim, and Staying That Way

Out of 1.1 million people served this month, almost none of them were ever asked for anything.

There is no paywall on prayer times. No subscription fee for the Qur’an. No charge to be reminded of Fajr. A rickshaw puller in Gazipur and a professional working in Dhaka open the exact same app, with the exact same access, side by side.

That is only possible because a small number of people quietly carry the cost for everybody else. That is what all of our contributors do, every single month, without recognition and without asking for any.

The Road Ahead

There is still real work in front of this project, and it’s worth being transparent about what that looks like.

iPhone users are still barely served. Almost everyone reached today is on Android, which means many brothers and sisters overseas – especially across the Gulf and Europe, where iPhone usage skews much higher – are not yet properly reached at all. Bringing the app to full strength on iPhone is the next major undertaking.

More languages, more places. France, Germany, Brazil, Indonesia, and India are already among the countries using the app organically, without any dedicated outreach. Proper translation and locally accurate prayer times would open the door far wider for comparatively little additional cost.

A faster, simpler app. Older screens are being consolidated so that every user – including those on slow connections and older, budget phones – gets the same clean, reliable experience regardless of where they live or what device they can afford.

Continued support from all of our contributors is what turns each of these from a stated intention into a released update that actually reaches someone at Fajr tomorrow.
